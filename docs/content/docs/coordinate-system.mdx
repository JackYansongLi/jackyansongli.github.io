In vector calculus and tensor analysis, **coordinate systems** are foundational for describing geometric space and physical quantities. This note introduces basis vectors, coordinate axes, and covariant/contravariant bases in three-dimensional space, along with an interactive 3D visualization.

## 1. Basic Concepts

In 3D Euclidean space, any point's position can be identified by coordinates $(x^1, x^2, x^3)$. To describe vectors, we establish a set of **basis vectors**:

- **Tangent Basis Vectors (Covariant Basis $\boldsymbol{e}_i$)**: Vectors tangent to coordinate curves.
- **Normal Basis Vectors (Contravariant Basis $\boldsymbol{e}^i$)**: Vectors normal to coordinate surfaces.

When coordinate axes are mutually perpendicular with unit length, they form a **Cartesian coordinate system**. In general curvilinear or oblique coordinates, basis vectors are not necessarily orthogonal or unit length.

## 3. Cross Product and Orthogonal Basis

Given two non-collinear basis vectors $\boldsymbol{e}_1$ and $\boldsymbol{e}_2$ in 3D space, we can uniquely determine a basis vector $\boldsymbol{e}^3$ orthogonal to the plane formed by $\boldsymbol{e}_1$ and $\boldsymbol{e}_2$ using the cross product:

$$
\boldsymbol{e}^3 \propto \boldsymbol{e}_1 \times \boldsymbol{e}_2
$$

This ensures that $\boldsymbol{e}^3$ is strictly perpendicular to the $Ox_1x_2$ plane:

$$
\boldsymbol{e}^3 \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_1 = 0, \quad \boldsymbol{e}^3 \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_2 = 0
$$

This construction is key in general curvilinear coordinates and tensor analysis for defining dual bases (contravariant bases).
